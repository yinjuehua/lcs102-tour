The battle for Okinawa ran from April 1 to June 22, 1945, and radar picket groups guarded the fleet. A radar picket was a ship, or a small group of ships, placed far from the main fleet. Its job was to spot enemy aircraft early and to guide friendly fighter planes toward them, a task called fighter direction.

Destroyers led the picket groups, and LCS gunboats supported them when they could. The gunboats watched the sky, fired at attackers, and rescued sailors from the water. Picket duty was dangerous because the stations were far apart and help was far away.

Kamikaze aircraft attacked the pickets again and again. A kamikaze aircraft was a plane that its pilot crashed into a ship on purpose. On April 12, 1945, a kamikaze struck LCS-33, and she burned, exploded, and sank. Other sister ships were lost at Okinawa too.

LCS-102 arrived at Okinawa on June 18, 1945, and her radar-picket duty began on July 10, 1945. Her action reports do not record her firing her guns in combat.

Her duty was still real service, standing watch far from the main fleet in the last weeks of the war.

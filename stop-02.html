The Navy needed a shallow, heavily armed gunboat that could go with the landing craft all the way to the beach. Its job was to keep firing so the troops were protected as they landed.

The Navy called this ship type Landing Craft Support, which means a small warship built to support troops landing on a beach. One hundred thirty of them were built during the war.

LCS-102 was built by Commercial Iron Works in Portland, Oregon. Her keel was laid in January 1945, and she was launched on February 3, 1945. She was commissioned on February 17, 1945, which means she was formally placed into active Navy service.

She was 158 feet long, and her assigned crew was six officers and 65 enlisted sailors.

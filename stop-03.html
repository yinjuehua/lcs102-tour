A ship like LCS-102 carried six officers and 65 enlisted men. Before a ship got her crew, the Navy turned young men into sailors at boot camp and then at trade schools. Each sailor learned a rating, which is the Navy's term for an enlisted job.

Every sailor trained for both a regular Navy job and an emergency job. Some stood signal watch on the bridge, identified other ships by their flags, and operated blinker lights and searchlights. Others operated and maintained the ship's radio transmitting and receiving equipment, sent and received Morse code, and enciphered and deciphered messages.

Each sailor also had a battle station, the place where he reported during an attack.

One of those sailors was Eddie Desmond, who served aboard LCS-128, a sister ship of LCS-102. His rating was ship's cook, and his battle station was a 20-millimeter anti-aircraft gun. An anti-aircraft gun is a gun designed to fire at enemy aircraft.
